    PRESENTATION STRATEGY & FORMAT GUIDE¶

    How to Present Your Premium UHNW Service¶

    Purpose: Different audiences need different information. This guide provides presentation formats for different stakeholders and contexts.

    I. PRESENTATION FORMATS BY AUDIENCE¶

    AUDIENCE 1: Internal Team / Board of Directors¶

    Context: Secure buy-in, budget approval, resource allocation
    Time: 30–45 minutes
    Format: PowerPoint deck + financial detail

    What they need to see: 1. Market opportunity (€11.7B segment, growth trajectory) 2. Competitive advantage (why we win vs. competitors) 3. Business model (simple, repeatable, profitable) 4. Financial projections (investment required, ROI timeline) 5. Go-to-market plan (first 90 days, key milestones) 6. Risk mitigation (what could go wrong, how we handle it) 7. Resource requirements (budget, staffing, timeline) 8. Key decisions needed (approval, budget, staffing)

    Deck structure: - Slide 1: "Premium UHNW Travel Service" (title) - Slide 2: Market size & growth (market opportunity) - Slide 3: Competitive landscape (why we win) - Slide 4: Business model overview (membership + commission) - Slide 5: Target clients (15 prospects, tier breakdown) - Slide 6: Financial projections (Year 1: €138K revenue, €52K profit) - Slide 7: 90-day roadmap (path to first client) - Slide 8: Staffing & budget (€35K startup + €4.2K/month) - Slide 9: Risk dashboard (mitigation strategies) - Slide 10: Decision framework (approvals needed)

    Key takeaway message: "High-margin service leveraging existing infrastructure. €52K profit Year 1 with 8 clients. Break-even Month 4–5. Defensible competitive advantage (Basque insider positioning)."

    AUDIENCE 2: First Potential Client (Warm Intro)¶

    Context: Initial relationship-building, gauge interest
    Time: 15–30 minutes (coffee/lunch)
    Format: Conversation + one-pager + proposal follow-up

    What they need to hear: 1. Relevance (why this matters to them specifically) 2. Insight (you understand their world, pain points) 3. Possibility (what's possible for them that's not elsewhere) 4. Ease (simple, not complicated, you handle the details) 5. Social proof (other similar clients, examples) 6. Next step (easy decision: "let me design a custom proposal")

    Conversation flow (not a pitch): 1. Opening (2 min): "Thanks for meeting. [Mutual contact] thought you'd appreciate what we're building." 2. Context (3 min): "We've been seeing ultra-wealthy Bilbao families want experiences that balance work life with family time. You can't get that off-the-shelf." 3. Insight (5 min): "For someone in your position—leading Gestamp globally, managing family legacy—what would it look like if you could design a multi-generational journey that brought your kids/grandkids together without disrupting your business?" 4. Possibility (5 min): Share one relevant example journey (e.g., Japan industrial tour + family bonding) 5. Ease (3 min): "We handle all the complexity. You just decide where and who goes. We do the research, booking, logistics, and even support you during the trip." 6. Close (2 min): "What if I spent a few hours researching what might work for your family? No commitment. Just a custom idea."

    Materials to bring: - ✅ One-pager (positioning statement + value prop) - ✅ Case study (anonymized example from another luxury travel provider, or hypothetical) - ✅ Magazine/visuals (Bilbao-focused lifestyle magazine, luxury travel examples) - ❌ Don't bring: Pricing, term sheets, contracts (too aggressive)

    Key takeaway message: "I understand your world. I can design experiences you can't get elsewhere. Let me show you what's possible."

    AUDIENCE 3: Potential Partnership (Hotel, Destination Operator)¶

    Context: Negotiate commission structure, volume commitments
    Time: 20–30 minutes (phone or meeting)
    Format: Pitch deck + partnership proposal

    What they need to see: 1. Volume opportunity (how many clients you'll send) 2. Client quality (UHNW = high spend, good reviews, repeat business) 3. Distribution channel (you're direct access to affluent market) 4. Profitability (your partnership drives their revenue) 5. Terms (commission rates, booking process, exclusivity) 6. Support (marketing, training, systems)

    Pitch structure: 1. Intro (2 min): "We're building a premium concierge service for UHNW residents of Bilbao. Our first target is multi-generational family journeys to Japan/Europe/Africa. We'll send 20–30 bookings/year." 2. Client profile (2 min): "Average booking: €45K per person. 8–12 participants per journey. Annual frequency: 1–2 times. Loyalty: 70%+ repeat rate. Reviews: 4.8+/5.0 (we're a screened referral service)." 3. Opportunity (2 min): "Every client you receive from us will spend 10–14 nights at your property. Multi-generational trips = longer stays, higher spend. They'll rave about the experience to their networks (referral loop)." 4. Partnership terms (3 min): - Commission: 10–15% on room rates (vs. 3–5% for travel agents) - Complimentary upgrades (room category) - Daily credits (€100–200 per room for spa/dining) - Concierge level service (we coordinate with your team) - Exclusivity: Yes, we'll feature you prominently in our destination options 5. Timeline (2 min): "We start with soft launch (5–10 bookings Q1). Scale to 20–30/quarter by Q3." 6. Next step (1 min): "Can we schedule a follow-up call with your regional director to finalize terms?"

    Materials to bring: - ✅ Partnership proposal (1-page terms) - ✅ Client profile summary (who they'll serve) - ✅ Booking process flowchart (how it works) - ✅ References (other concierge services or luxury partners) - ❌ Don't bring: Everything at once (overwhelming)

    Key takeaway message: "We're a quality distribution channel for your premium rooms. Our clients are perfect—affluent, loyal, good reviews. Let's partnership."

    AUDIENCE 4: Potential Investor / Advisor¶

    Context: Evaluate business model, growth potential
    Time: 45–60 minutes
    Format: Pitch deck + financial model

    What they need to see: 1. Market size & TAM (addressable market—is it big enough?) 2. Unit economics (margin per client, LTV, CAC) 3. Growth trajectory (Year 1, 2, 3 projections) 4. Competitive moat (defensibility, barriers to entry) 5. Management team (who's running it, track record) 6. Go-to-market strategy (how you'll acquire clients) 7. Use of funds (what are you raising for) 8. Exit/return potential (how does investor make money)

    Pitch deck structure: - Slide 1: "Premium UHNW Travel Service—Market Opportunity" - Slide 2: TAM (€11.7B Spain luxury segment, 2,500 UHNW Bilbao households) - Slide 3: Unit economics (€29K revenue/client, 35–40% margins, €25K LTV) - Slide 4: Growth model (8 clients Y1 → 14 Y2 → 20+ Y3) - Slide 5: Competitive advantage (Basque insider + global network) - Slide 6: Go-to-market (warm intros → referrals → events) - Slide 7: Financial projections (Year 1–3, revenue & profit) - Slide 8: Team (who's leading, relevant experience) - Slide 9: Use of funds (what you're raising for, allocation) - Slide 10: Return potential (investor exit/return scenarios)

    Specific financial metrics to highlight: - CAC (customer acquisition cost): €500–1,000 - LTV (lifetime value): €25,000–30,000 - LTV/CAC ratio: 25–30x (exceptional) - Payback period: <6 months - Gross margin: 35–40% - Scaling efficiency: Revenue grows 3x while costs grow 1.5x

    Key takeaway message: "Exceptional unit economics + recurring revenue + defensible niche = attractive investment. Path to €300K+ annual profit by Year 3."

    AUDIENCE 5: Sales Kickoff / Marketing Team¶

    Context: Align team on positioning, target, messaging
    Time: 1–2 hours
    Format: Workshop + briefing materials

    What they need to know: 1. Target profile (who we're after, beyond demographics) 2. Positioning (how we're different, key message) 3. Value proposition (why they should choose us) 4. Messaging (language, tone, key phrases) 5. Sales process (how to approach, qualification, close) 6. Objection handling (common questions, answers) 7. Stories & examples (client journeys, value demonstrations) 8. Tools & materials (one-pagers, case studies, proposals)

    Workshop agenda: 1. Discovery (15 min): What are key characteristics of our ideal client? 2. Positioning (15 min): Why should they choose us vs. alternatives? 3. Messaging (15 min): What's our key message? How do we say it? 4. Process (15 min): How do we approach prospects? What's the sales flow? 5. Tactics (30 min): Warm intros vs. cold outreach, events, networking 6. Q&A & scenarios (30 min): Role-play sales conversations, handle objections

    Sales playbook to distribute: - Target profile (15 detailed prospects) - Positioning statement - Key value propositions (3–5 main benefits) - Common objections & responses - Sales email templates - Proposal template - Closing checklist

    Key takeaway message: "We're not competing on price or breadth. We're competing on insider knowledge + personalization + exclusive access. That's our selling point."

    AUDIENCE 6: Public / Media (If Positioning Publicly)¶

    Context: Build brand awareness, attract clients
    Time: 5–10 minutes (article, video, podcast)
    Format: Story-driven narrative

    Story angle: "Local travel expert bridges gap for Bilbao's ultra-wealthy. While international agencies offer generic luxury, one local agency understands Basque values—family legacy, work ethic, meaningful experiences—and designs bespoke journeys that matter. We talked to the founder about how understanding culture is the real luxury."

    Key messages for media: - "Travel isn't about the hotel. It's about meaning." - "Basque business culture values relationships above everything. That's how we serve." - "Exclusive access isn't about expensive. It's about authentic." - "Our clients don't want to be sold. They want to be understood."

    Story examples for media: - Example 1: "Multi-generational family journey to Japan—combined father's business mentorship with family bonding." (anonymized) - Example 2: "Art collector gets private viewings at three continents—access competitors can't offer." - Example 3: "Sustainability executive designs expedition combining innovation research with conservation impact."

    Don't mention: Pricing, specific clients, internal operations

    Key takeaway message: "Luxury travel is becoming more human. Billionaires don't want more hotels; they want more meaning."

    II. VISUAL PRESENTATION FORMATS¶

    FORMAT A: PowerPoint Deck (Professional, Formal)¶

    Use for: Board presentations, investor pitches, partnership negotiations
    Structure: - Title slide - 1-page executive summary (key metrics) - Market opportunity (size, growth, trends) - Competitive landscape (positioning matrix) - Business model (revenue streams, pricing) - Target customers (15-client overview) - Service offerings (6 journey types) - Go-to-market strategy (90-day roadmap) - Financial projections (revenue, profit, margin) - Risk & mitigation - Decision/next steps - Appendix (detailed data)

    Design tips: - Minimize text (headlines + supporting graphics) - Use data visualization (charts, matrices, dashboards) - Consistent branding (logo, colors, fonts) - One idea per slide - High-quality imagery (luxury travel, Bilbao, Basque culture)

    FORMAT B: One-Pager (Concise, Punchy)¶

    Use for: Client first meetings, partnership pitches, networking events
    Structure (Front side): - Headline: "Bespoke luxury journeys for Bilbao's ultra-wealthy" - Positioning: "Basque insider expertise + global network access" - Three value props: 1. "Exclusive access to experiences unavailable elsewhere" 2. "Personalized to your lifestyle, interests, values" 3. "White-glove service—we handle all complexity" - Sample journey: 1 example (legacy journey, art tour, etc.) - Contact info

    Structure (Back side): - About us: 50-word company description - Why choose us: 3–4 key differentiators - Client testimonial: (once you have one) - Next step: "Let's design your custom journey"

    FORMAT C: Email Pitch (Direct, Conversational)¶

    Use for: Cold outreach (rarely), warm intros, partnership inquiries
    Structure: - Subject line: "A custom journey for [Client Name]" (personalized) - Greeting: "Hi [Name], [Mutual friend] suggested we connect…" - Hook (1–2 sentences): Insight into their world/pain point - Idea (2–3 sentences): What's possible for them - Ease (1 sentence): How simple it is to get started - CTA (1 sentence): "Could we grab coffee to explore?" - Signature: Name, title, contact info

    Example email: 

    Subject: Japan legacy journey for [Gestamp family]?

Hi Francisco,

[Friend] mentioned you're passionate about preserving family legacy while building the company. That's a tension we see often—wanting to bond generations without disrupting business.

We've designed multi-generational journeys that balance both. One example: 2 weeks in Japan (Hokkaido)—your kids see how the business works (manufacturing tours), everyone bonds (hot springs, skiing), and we document it as a family archive.

No pitch needed—just curious if this resonates. Could we grab a 20-minute coffee?

Best,
[Your name]
[Your title]
[Your phone]

    FORMAT D: Interactive Dashboard (Data-Driven, Real-Time)¶

    Use for: Internal tracking, partner metrics, client insights
    Tools: Google Sheets, Tableau, Excel, Klipboard
    Sections: - Monthly revenue tracker (actual vs. plan) - Client pipeline (intro → proposal → booking) - Key metrics (NPS, retention, LTV) - Partnership performance (commission, volume) - Marketing spend vs. ROI - Operational metrics (proposal close rate, delivery quality)

    Update frequency: Weekly or monthly

    FORMAT E: Case Study / Story (Narrative, Emotional)¶

    Use for: Marketing content, client credibility, media interviews
    Structure: 1. Client introduction (anonymized): Who they are, business, lifestyle 2. Challenge: What they were struggling with (time, meaningful travel, exclusive access) 3. Discovery conversation: How we understood their world 4. Solution: The custom journey we designed 5. Execution: How we made it happen (logistics, partnerships, concierge) 6. Outcome: What happened, how it impacted them 7. Quote: Client testimonial (if permission granted)

    Example opening: "When we met [Client], she was burned out. Leading [company] globally, managing [family], trying to keep [kids] connected to [values]. Travel was another item on the to-do list, not a lifeline. We knew we could change that."

    III. PRESENTATION BY CHANNEL¶

    CHANNEL 1: In-Person Meeting (15–30 min)¶

    Best for: First client meetings, partnership negotiations
    Preparation: - Print one-pager (high-quality, professional) - Bring digital portfolio (iPad with images) - Have proposal framework ready - Wear smart casual (professional but not stuffy)

    Delivery: - Conversation, not monologue - Ask questions first, listen more than talk - Share relevant example (tailored to their interests) - Propose next step gently ("What if I researched…?")

    CHANNEL 2: Zoom/Video Call (20–45 min)¶

    Best for: Remote partnerships, geographically dispersed clients
    Preparation: - Clean, professional background - Share screen with deck/proposal - Send deck 24 hours before (they'll have context) - Test tech 10 min early

    Delivery: - Eye contact (look at camera, not screen) - Clear, deliberate speech (slower than in-person) - Share screen for visuals - Engage: "What are your thoughts on this approach?"

    CHANNEL 3: Email / Written (Asynchronous)¶

    Best for: Busy executives, initial contact
    Preparation: - Short, scannable (3–4 paragraphs max) - Personalized (reference specific interests) - One clear CTA (coffee? Call? Response?) - Professional but friendly tone

    Delivery: - Subject line matters (curiosity, not spam) - Lead with their need/interest - End with easy yes/no question - Follow up in 1 week if no response

    CHANNEL 4: Event / Networking (10–20 min)¶

    Best for: Finding new prospects, building brand
    Preparation: - Business cards ready - 30-second elevator pitch memorized - Conversation starters (ask about their business) - Listen for pain points matching our solution

    Delivery: - Find 3–4 meaningful conversations (not work the room) - Ask more than tell - Exchange cards naturally - Follow up within 48 hours (email or lunch invite)

    CHANNEL 5: Public Speaking / Thought Leadership¶

    Best for: Positioning as expert, building credibility
    Format options: - Panel at luxury business conference - Podcast interview on travel/business - Article in luxury lifestyle magazine - Roundtable discussion on family legacy

    Key messages: - Travel is undervalued as family bonding tool - Basque values (family, work ethic) inspire journey design - UHNW clients want meaning, not just luxury - Local expertise + global access = defensible advantage

    IV. PRESENTATION TIMELINE STRATEGY¶

    T-MINUS 1 WEEK (Before Major Presentation)¶

    • ✅ Draft deck/materials
    • ✅ Send to internal reviewer (get feedback)
    • ✅ Rehearse out loud (catch awkward phrasing)
    • ✅ Prepare financial backup slides (not in main deck)
    • ✅ Create one-pager for leave-behind

    T-MINUS 1 DAY¶

    • ✅ Finalize slides (no last-minute changes)
    • ✅ Test all technology (projector, video, internet)
    • ✅ Print materials (if in-person)
    • ✅ Lay out talking points (per slide)
    • ✅ Get 8 hours of sleep (you'll be sharper)

    DURING PRESENTATION¶

    • ✅ Arrive 15 min early (settle nerves, test tech)
    • ✅ Start with clear objective ("Here's what I want you to remember...")
    • ✅ Pause for questions (don't rush)
    • ✅ Use examples/stories (data is boring; stories stick)
    • ✅ End with clear CTA (decision, next step)

    AFTER PRESENTATION¶

    • ✅ Send promised materials within 24 hours
    • ✅ Thank them (email, handwritten note)
    • ✅ Reference specific points they raised ("Following up on your question about...")
    • ✅ Propose next step explicitly (meeting? Call? Decision date?)

    V. QUICK REFERENCE: WHAT TO PRESENT, BY CONTEXT¶

    Context Time Key Slides Tone
    First client meeting 20 min Positioning + 1 example journey + ease Consultative, not salesy
    Board approval 30 min Market + model + projections + decision Confident, data-driven
    Partnership negotiation 25 min Volume + client quality + terms Professional, mutually beneficial
    Investor pitch 45 min TAM + unit econ + growth + team + returns Compelling, realistic
    Marketing kickoff 90 min Target + positioning + messaging + process Collaborative, workshop-style
    Media interview 10 min Story angle + key message (no slides) Conversational, authentic
    Networking event 10 min Elevator pitch + business card (no slides) Friendly, question-focused

    VI. MESSAGING HIERARCHY¶

    PRIMARY MESSAGE (Lead with this)¶

    "We design bespoke journeys for Bilbao's ultra-wealthy, combining deep local knowledge with global exclusive access."

    SECONDARY MESSAGES (Support the primary)¶

    1. "Basque insider expertise that competitors lack"
    2. "Personalized to lifestyle, values, and pain points"
    3. "Exclusive experiences unavailable to general public"
    4. "White-glove concierge service—we handle complexity"
    5. "Membership model = recurring relationship, not transactional"

    SUPPORTING PROOF POINTS¶

    • 2,500 UHNW prospects in Bilbao
    • €11.7B annual luxury travel market
    • No competitor focused on "Bilbao UHNW specialist"
    • Existing partnerships with Four Seasons, Rosewood, etc.
    • Proven delivery model (case studies from similar services)

    FINAL CHECKLIST: BEFORE YOU PRESENT¶

    PRESENTATION READINESS CHECKLIST

Deck/Materials
  ☐ Slides proofread (no typos)
  ☐ Data accurate (no outdated numbers)
  ☐ Visuals professional (high-quality images)
  ☐ One-pager printed (if in-person)
  ☐ Leave-behind materials ready

Delivery
  ☐ Talking points prepared (per slide)
  ☐ Practiced out loud (sounds natural)
  ☐ Time-checked (within limit)
  ☐ Tech tested (projector, audio, internet)
  ☐ Backup plan (if tech fails)

You
  ☐ Business appropriate attire
  ☐ 8 hours sleep (you'll think clearer)
  ☐ Ate breakfast (low blood sugar = poor thinking)
  ☐ Silence phone
  ☐ Confident mindset (you know this stuff)

Environment
  ☐ Arrive 15 min early
  ☐ Greet warmly (smile, eye contact)
  ☐ Seat people strategically (key decision-maker in view)
  ☐ Water available
  ☐ No interruptions (silence notifications)

Post-Presentation
  ☐ Thank them explicitly
  ☐ Send promised materials within 24 hours
  ☐ Follow-up call scheduled (if appropriate)
  ☐ Note any action items
  ☐ Update CRM with outcomes
